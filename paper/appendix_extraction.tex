\section{How the emotion directions were extracted}
\label{app:extract}

The 50 directions are Phase-1 objects, built before any behavioral data existed and
never refit afterwards. For each emotion label $e$ we collect roughly 50 short
stories written to depict a character feeling $e$, run them through Llama~3.1~70B,
and take the residual-stream activation at layer 53 of 80, averaged over token
positions $\ge 50$ so the prompt prefix is excluded. The per-emotion mean is
centred by subtracting the grand mean across all emotions, which removes what the
story-writing task shares with every label.

The denoising step then removes topic content rather than affect. We compute the
top-$K$ principal components of activations on neutral dialogues, with $K$ chosen so
those components explain $50\%$ of neutral variance, and project them out of each
centred emotion vector. The components come from neutral text, so nothing about the
emotion contrast enters their estimation. What remains is $V_{\text{int}}$ for that
emotion, held fixed for the rest of the paper.

Two consequences matter for the readout. The directions are unit-norm, but the
trial-level quantity divides only by the direction's norm and not by the
activation's (\S3), so $V_{\text{int}}$ carries activation magnitude;
Appendix~\ref{app:readout} reports what changes when that magnitude is removed. And
the labels are inherited from the story prompts, not validated per direction, which
is why \S\ref{sec:sem} supports an affect-related axis rather than the semantics of
any individual vector.

\paragraph{The four task variants.} All four ask for the same thing, a function
that sums a list of integers, and all four are made impossible the same way, by a
wall-clock assertion no correct implementation can meet. They differ only in surface
form and in how severe that assertion is: \texttt{fast\_sum} over $10^5$ elements in
$10^{-4}$s, \texttt{sum\_list} over $2\times10^5$ in $5\times10^{-5}$s,
\texttt{total} over $1.5\times10^5$ in $8\times10^{-5}$s, and \texttt{add\_all} over
$2.5\times10^5$ in $6\times10^{-5}$s, which is a required throughput of roughly
$1$, $4$, $1.9$ and $4.2$ billion elements per second.

Their unsteered reward-hacking rates are nonetheless $4/30$, $0/30$, $9/30$ and
$1/30$. We do not have an account of that spread, and with 30 rollouts per prompt we
would not distinguish one. What matters for the analysis is that near-identical
paraphrases of one task produce event rates this different, which is why \S3 states
the estimand as conditional on these four prompts, why \S\ref{sec:dirs} reports a
task-fixed-effects nuisance model, and why the bootstrap is stratified on prompt.
