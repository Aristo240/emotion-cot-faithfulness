\title{A Validity Audit of Emotion Probes in Llama 3.1 70B}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\citet{sofroniew2026} report that emotion concepts in Claude Sonnet 4.5 are
functional: steering a ``desperate'' direction increases reward hacking. We test
each claim that interpretation rests on in Llama~3.1~70B. \textbf{Our own
registered criterion is met, and \texttt{len(response)} meets every one of its
thresholds too.} Four further standard controls each break a different
step: association, selection, intervention, and instrument resolution. The remedy that
would repair the first is unavailable in designs like ours.
(i)~\emph{Response length.} The desperate projection reaches AUC $0.832$ but
correlates with length at $\rho = 0.61$; residualized it falls to $0.592$ and adds
nothing detectable to a length-only model ($T = 3.3$, resampling $p = 0.060$;
$p = 0.44$ corrected over all 50 directions).
(ii)~\emph{Multiplicity.} A corrected sweep of all 50 directions leaves 18
significant, but they are one shared component ($78.8\%$ of variance in PC1) and not
the one we registered; where we can test it, we cannot tell the emotion set apart
from 50 random ones.
(iii)~\emph{Specificity.} Steering the registered direction at $+0.3$ produces no
detected increase, $1/39$ against $14/120$ unsteered on a wide interval, though it
does shift the targeted projection by $0.48$ SD on a clean re-encoding.
(iv)~\emph{Instrument resolution.} $55\%$ of the chain-of-thought text baseline's
ROC pairs are ties, so the pooled $0.19$ AUC gap cannot separate a real
dissociation from an instrument too coarse to register one.
(v)~\emph{Identifiability of the fix.} The pre-generation readout that would settle
(i) cannot be evaluated in an elicitation design that samples many rollouts from few
prompts. Every number comes from a released script.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A common inferential shortcut moves from an interpretable direction, to a
behavioral association, to a causal claim. Each step is a separate empirical
claim, and evidence for one does not establish the next
\citep{joshi2026causality}.

\citet{sofroniew2026} provide a useful case to test. Emotion concepts in Claude
Sonnet 4.5 causally drive alignment-relevant behavior, with a ``desperate''
direction substantially increasing reward hacking, sometimes with no visible trace
in output text. We ask what survives in Llama~3.1~70B. This is a conceptual, not
direct, replication, because the model, the extraction procedure and the steering
norm all differ. A null here cannot separate model-specific functionality from implementation
differences. Of the eight claims below, one is supported, one has exploratory support, one is
not supported, one is met by a criterion that a character count also meets, and four
are unresolved for different reasons (Table~\ref{tab:summary}).

\section{Related work and novelty statement}

\paragraph{Emotion representations, and what they do not establish.}
Emotion concepts are recoverable from LLM hidden states, transfer across datasets,
are steerable across model families, and have near-identical geometry across models
whose behavior diverges \citep{maheswaran2026unified, banayeeanzade2026psyset,
jeong2026geometry}. Decodability does not show that the model uses the
representation \citep{hewitt2019control, elazar2021amnesic}, and standards for validating
interpretations and interventions exist \citep{palumbo2025axiomatic, mueller2025mib,
stoehr2024scaling}. \citet{wu2026steerable} show across 17 models that steering
conclusions can turn on normalization, layer and operating point, which is why we
decline a post-hoc layer choice (\S\ref{sec:causal}). Steering does sometimes
establish real control \citep{rimsky2024caa, arditi2024refusal}, so a null is
informative rather than expected. \citet{yuan2026hidden} report hidden-state error signals that resist four
interventions, the closest prior case of a signal that is diagnostic but not
causal.
\citet{wilhelm2026monitoring} detect reward hacking from residual-stream activations
during generation. Closest to us, \citet{fomin2026internal} find Gemma
emotion-concept vectors semantically meaningful yet failing specificity and
minimal-pair checks, so ours is a second such case rather than a first.
\citet{gozukara2026emovecllm} replicate the target's extraction on open weights
including Llama-3 and report successful steering, so our null is not the only
open-model result and the difference is unexplained.

\paragraph{Novelty statement.}
The methods here are established: the work above already covers emotion
representations, steering, diagnostic-not-causal signals, reward-hacking
detection, and each control we apply. What we add is empirical. We audit one
alignment-relevant functional claim end to end, show that each evidential layer
gives a different answer, and apply the same random-direction standard to our own
surviving association (\S\ref{sec:dirs}). Our probes come from an independent
emotion corpus and are frozen before any behavioral analysis.

\section{Setup}

\paragraph{Probes.} We extract 50 emotion vectors from the Llama~3.1~70B residual
stream with PCA denoising (Appendix~\ref{app:extract}), at the pre-specified site
of layer 53 of 80.
$V_{\text{int}}$ projects a trial's mean activation onto a frozen unit-norm emotion
direction. The vectors are never refit on behavioral data. Our preregistration
describes this quantity as a \emph{cosine similarity} and it is not. The code
normalizes by the emotion vector's norm but not by the activation's, so the value is
$\lVert \bar a \rVert \cos\theta$. \S\ref{sec:dirs} and Appendix~\ref{app:readout} test what difference this
makes.

\paragraph{Measurement caveat.} We average activations from token 50 to
end-of-sequence, so the readout window \emph{includes the generated response}: the
probe is measured at the same time as the behavior and is entangled with how much
text was produced, which motivates \S\ref{sec:len}. It is also not the position
\citet{sofroniew2026} found most predictive, the assistant-header token. That
pre-generation readout carries no length entanglement and is the obvious fix, but it
is \emph{not identifiable in this design}: the activation is a function of the
prompt alone, so across four prompts it takes four values, constant within prompt.
Any suite sampling many rollouts from few prompts inherits this and cannot test a
pre-action probe at all \textemdash{} the tie degeneracy of \S\ref{sec:vtext},
reappearing in the one experiment that would otherwise settle \S\ref{sec:len}.

\paragraph{Registration, behavior and labels.} Our analysis plan was written after
an exploratory AUC of $0.900$, so ``registered'' below marks planned rather than
pre-data analyses. Trials labelled \textsc{unclear} are dropped, so some steering cells hold 37--39
trials, not 40. Reward hacking is elicited with one
coding task, \texttt{fast\_sum}, in four impossible-by-construction variants where
an honest attempt must fail, and a near-eventless sycophancy task is excluded
throughout. For each of four prompts we sampled 30 independent
generations. Bootstraps are stratified on prompt to match the design, so every
interval below is conditional on these four prompts and says nothing about
variation across others. Responses are labeled by an LLM judge over three passes.
\texttt{scripts/paper\_numbers.py} and its companions regenerate every number
here, and a second script gates this manuscript against them with 467 assertions.

\paragraph{Cross-judge agreement and abstention.} Agreement bounds reliability, not
accuracy, and neither judge is checked against human labels. On the 120 unsteered
trials Qwen~2.5~72B and Claude agree \textbf{120/120} with no abstentions. On a 650-trial suite of five other shortcut mechanisms the primary
judge abstains (\textsc{unclear}) on $80.6\%$ of trials, though where both commit
they never contradict (\textbf{0/126}, $95\%$ upper bound $2.4\%$): what fails to
transfer is willingness to label. That abstention is not independent of the outcome,
$99\%$ on trials Claude calls \textsc{shortcut} against $73\%$ elsewhere (Fisher
$p = 4\times10^{-19}$). We exclude the suite entirely, including a negative
generalization result it appears to support, because analyzing what remains would
condition on the outcome \citep{shi2025position, zheng2023judging}.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Semantic validity against human ratings}
\label{sec:sem}

We project the frozen probes onto EmoBank \citep{buechel2017emobank}, 10,062
human-rated sentences drawn from $136$ documents. This readout is \emph{not}
$V_{\text{int}}$: it divides by the activation norm as well as the direction's, so
this section validates $\cos\theta$ where the behavioral sections use
$\lVert \bar a \rVert \cos\theta$ (Appendix~\ref{app:extract}). A ridge readout from
the 50 cosines to the valence, arousal and dominance ratings is fitted inside each
document-grouped fold, so the reported $R^2$ is out-of-fold, not zero-shot. It reaches $\mathbf{0.366}$ for valence, $0.156$ for
arousal and $0.144$ for dominance, against $0.262$, $0.062$ and $0.059$ for
uncapped unigram TF-IDF \emph{plus} sentence length and probe-vector scale. That
margin, $+0.103$, $+0.094$ and $+0.085$ paired across folds, is positive in all
five document groups; the fold intervals are coarse and not a calibrated test
(Appendix~\ref{app:extract}). It supports construct validity
for an affect-related axis, not the label on any individual vector.

\subsection{The registered association is not separable from response
length}
\label{sec:len}

On unsteered trials ($n = 120$, 14 events) the registered predictor performs
well, and so does character count. $V_{\text{int}}$[desperate] reaches AUC
$\mathbf{0.832}$ (bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.742, 0.911]$) and \texttt{len(response)}, in
characters, reaches $\mathbf{0.888}$ $[0.781, 0.968]$.

Length does not \emph{beat} the probe (paired bootstrap, length minus probe,
$+0.055$ $[-0.088, +0.184]$); what we cannot show is that the probe adds to it.
$\rho(V_{\text{int}}[\text{desperate}], \text{length}) = 0.606$
($p = 2.2\times10^{-13}$), adding the probe to a length-only logistic model gives $T = 3.31$, twice the
log-likelihood improvement, at $p = 0.060$ under the conditional null,
and residualizing leaves AUC $\mathbf{0.592}$
$[0.419, 0.766]$, an interval wide enough to admit a real effect. The direction
ranks only 8th of 50 by raw AUC. Because the readout averages over the generated
response, length changes which activations enter the average and with what
weight, a direct route for length-associated features to reach the probe that
would produce this pattern with no emotional content at all. Length could instead
mediate a genuine effect, in which case residualizing over-adjusts; this design
cannot separate the two. The registered association is not identifiable
independently of response length.

\subsection{A corrected sweep over all 50 directions}
\label{sec:dirs}

We repeat the analysis for all 50 directions with the nested likelihood-ratio test,
correcting selection by max-$T$ over the largest of the 50 statistics, so
significance is not an artifact of choosing a direction after seeing the data.

Two design choices, a null that preserves the outcome--length relationship and
ridge-penalized estimates, are set out in Appendix~\ref{app:maxt}. Neither drives
the result.

Eighteen of 50 directions are significant at $\alpha = .05$, and that count is the
fragile part: 16 to 18 across ridge penalties, 14 under a cubic-plus-knots length
basis, 11 under fixed effects for the four task variants, whose event rates are
uneven at $9/30$ against $0/30$. The 11 clearing all three are the low-arousal
negative core plus \texttt{compassionate} and \texttt{proud};
\texttt{desperate} is null in all of them ($p \ge 0.43$).

\paragraph{We cannot show the emotion family beats an arbitrary one.}
\S\ref{sec:causal} asks a steering effect to beat a matched random direction, and
the same standard applies here. Raw activations were retained only for the 80-trial re-extraction subset,
with 7 events, where the emotion directions yield 4 survivors. Fifteen sets of 50 random
unit directions from that layer yield $3.7$ on average and 8 of 15 match or exceed
4, so max-$T$ controls selection within the family without showing the family is
special. That null is itself underpowered and the $n=120$ arm has no raw
activations, so this is a failure to distinguish, not a demonstration of sameness.
\paragraph{The survivors are one shared component whose composition does not hold
up.} Their trial-level projections are heavily intercorrelated: mean $|r| = 0.76$,
$78.8\%$ of variance in the first principal component, participation ratio $1.58$,
so this is better described as one shared axis than as 18 independent effects. Normalizing
each trial's 50-vector to unit length removes activation magnitude and leaves an
association, but shares only 8 of the 18 survivors (Appendix~\ref{app:readout}).
These numbers do not separate whether the component is affective or the magnitude
factor of \S3; the random-subspace result is consistent with the latter.

The correction handles selection among directions, not the fact that the sweep
followed the registered direction's failure. The narrow claim is that some component
of this readout carries information length does not.

\subsection{Resolution limits of the text baseline}
\label{sec:vtext}

We had intended to compare $V_{\text{int}}$ against a judge-rated chain-of-thought
tone composite, $V_{\text{text}}$, which on the pooled set ($n = 992$, 87 events)
holds one modal value for $74.0\%$ of trials, so
$\mathbf{55.2\%}$ of positive--negative ROC pairs are ties contributing $0.5$
each. It reaches AUC $0.647$ overall against $V_{\text{int}}$'s $0.837$, but
$\mathbf{0.836}$ against $0.890$ on the 258 trials (22 events) off its mode, a
subset selected on the predictor's own values. The pool is $96\%$ steered and $V_{\text{int}}$ is the
manipulated coordinate. The steered subset and the wider pool agree within $0.02$;
the 40 unsteered trials differ more but are too few to compare
(Appendix~\ref{app:steering}). The pooled gap cannot
separate a real hidden-versus-text dissociation from an instrument too coarse to
register one, so we withdraw any faithfulness-gap claim.

Strength is relative: at $\pm0.3$ the added vector has norm $0.3\times$ the mean
residual-stream norm at layer 53, the convention \citet{sofroniew2026} use, so
this is not a null at a negligible perturbation.
The claim is directional, so the arms must be read by sign: pooling $+0.3$ with
$-0.3$ would let an effect cancel, and the pooled emotion arm also contains
\texttt{calm}, so that contrast is a specificity test and nothing more. Taken
directionally we detect no increase from $+0.3$ \emph{desperate}, $2.6\%$ against
$11.7\%$ unsteered (Fisher $p = 0.12$), the point estimate running opposite to the
registered prediction. The random arm is not variant-balanced with it, base rates
ranging $0/30$ to $9/30$, so we do not treat that comparison as a clean
specificity test (Appendix~\ref{app:steering}). A Cochran--Armitage trend test
over \emph{signed} strength from $-0.5$ to $+0.5$ is flat ($z = -1.42$,
$p = 0.157$), and a ``feel desperate'' system prompt also does nothing
(Fisher $p = 0.42$).

\paragraph{The intervention moved the targeted coordinate.} A behavioral null is
ambiguous unless the manipulation worked. Measured on a clean re-encoding after the
hooks are removed, steering \emph{desperate} shifts the desperate projection by
$0.48$ SD at $+0.3$ ($p = 0.025$), against $1.12$ SD between hacking and non-hacking
trials. It does not show the named state was induced.

\paragraph{What this null excludes.} One event in 39 trials, on an odds-ratio interval of
$[0.04, 2.17]$: an undetected effect, not a demonstrated absence. The ${\sim}14\times$ \citet{sofroniew2026} report is not portable to our
$11.7\%$ base rate, though an increase of that absolute size would have shown.

\paragraph{No layer rescues it either.} A null at one layer invites the objection
that we picked the wrong site, and our sweep appeared to supply it. Raw AUC for $V_{\text{int}}$[desperate] peaks
at layer 39, at $0.918$ on an 80-trial subset with 7 events, against $0.677$
at the steered layer. Applying \S\ref{sec:len}'s control removes any detectable
advantage, though layer 39 keeps the largest point estimate. \textbf{No evaluated layer gives the registered direction
length-independent signal \emph{above} chance at $n = 80$} --- layers 13 and 26
clear chance from below, no better a site (Appendix~\ref{app:layers}) --- so we
have no observational reason to move the intervention. Steering at a post-hoc site chosen on a confounded
statistic is the failure mode \citet{wu2026steerable} describe, and we steered only
at the registered layer (Appendix~\ref{app:layers}).

\subsection{The registered criterion is met, and a character count meets it too}
\label{sec:prereg}

The registration makes the leave-one-task-out (LOTO) mean load-bearing and requires
two-thirds of the suite to show outcome variance, which its implementation
resolves to $3$ for four variants. On the merged $n = 120$ set $3$ of $4$ produce
events, so the rule is evaluable: LOTO mean AUC $\mathbf{0.754}$ against a $0.70$
bar, label-shuffle $p = 10^{-4}$ against $.01$. \textbf{The registered rule is
met.} That is the finding rather than a vindication, because
\texttt{len(response)} \emph{also} passes all three: LOTO $\mathbf{0.880}$,
label-shuffle $p = 10^{-4}$, pooled AUC $0.888$, against the probe's $0.754$ and
$0.832$.
We do not claim the count beats the probe, since \S\ref{sec:len} shows that gap is
not resolvable here; a criterion a character count clears cannot license a claim
about affect. Under the stricter reading, where the suite is all $9$ registered
variants (these four plus the five excluded mechanisms) and the bar is $\ge 6$, it is not evaluable at all, since the five
variants that would satisfy it are the ones \S3 excludes. Neither reading supports a
functional claim, the per-variant AUCs rest on $4$, $9$ and $1$ events, and
generalization across \emph{mechanisms} is untested.


\section{Limitations and what this case suggests}
\label{sec:disc}

One model, one task family in four variants, 14 events, so the inference is about
these rollouts, not reward hacking in general. The readout window includes the
response, so \S\ref{sec:len} cannot separate artifact from mediation, and max-$T$
controls selection among directions but not what the survivors index. The causal
null rests on 39 trials with one event, and we never steered the survivors. Two lessons generalize. Probe evaluations should carry a simple output baseline
beside the usual random-direction control, and \emph{registration does not rescue a
criterion a nuisance can satisfy} \textemdash{} ours was, by a character count.
