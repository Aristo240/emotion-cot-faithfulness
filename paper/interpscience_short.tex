\begin{abstract}
\citet{sofroniew2026} report that emotion concepts in Claude Sonnet 4.5 are
functional: steering a ``desperate'' direction causally increases reward hacking.
We audit whether that interpretation transfers to a large open-weight model, by
reconstructing emotion directions in Llama~3.1~70B and asking separately what
evidence supports each level of interpretation. Semantic validity holds: the
frozen directions recover human valence ratings on EmoBank zero-shot
(cross-validated $R^2 = 0.38$, $n = 10{,}062$). A behavioral association holds
within the task distribution: on unsteered trials the desperate projection
separates reward-hacking from non-hacking responses with AUC $0.832$
$[0.742, 0.909]$, unchanged under an independent cross-family rejudge of all 120
trials. But the preregistered generalization criterion is \emph{not} met, because
the mechanistically diverse task variants that the criterion requires did not
yield usable labels; the internal signal does \emph{not} significantly beat a
chain-of-thought text baseline at our pre-specified layer ($\Delta = 0.037$,
$p = 0.39$); and steering the emotion direction is statistically indistinguishable
from steering a random direction of matched norm ($7.5\%$ vs.\ $6.5\%$ hack rate,
Fisher $p = 0.835$). We also note that our probe averages the residual stream
across the response, so the association is concurrent rather than predictive. We
report the sequence in which our own initially strong conclusions weakened under
each added control, and release a claim-to-file provenance record. None of the
methodological principles here are new; the contribution is a concrete, auditable
case in which they bite.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A common inferential shortcut in interpretability runs: we found a direction that
is semantically interpretable; it tracks a behavior we care about; therefore it is
a mechanism, and steering it is a lever. Each link is a separate empirical claim,
and \citet{joshi2026causality} argue that interpretability claims routinely outrun
the level of causal evidence actually supplied.

\citet{sofroniew2026} provide an unusually crisp target for testing this. They
report that Claude Sonnet 4.5 forms linear representations of emotion concepts
that causally drive alignment-relevant behavior, with a ``desperate'' direction
substantially increasing reward hacking, and note that such shifts can occur with
no visible trace in the output text. We ask the natural follow-up: \emph{when this
result is transferred to a large open-weight model, which parts of the
interpretation survive which controls?}

We organize the audit into four progressively stronger validity questions
(Table~\ref{tab:ladder}). This organization is not itself novel --- it restates
distinctions already drawn by \citet{elazar2021amnesic}, \citet{stoehr2024scaling},
\citet{palumbo2025axiomatic}, \citet{mueller2025mib} and \citet{joshi2026causality}.
Our contribution is the audit: on the same probes, trials and labels, questions
1 and 2 are answered affirmatively, question 3 is answered negatively at the
pre-specified operating point, and question 4 is answered negatively outright.

\section{Related work and novelty statement}

\paragraph{Emotion representations and steering.}
Emotion concepts are recoverable from LLM hidden states across many models.
\citet{maheswaran2026unified} find emotion directions that transfer across
datasets; \citet{banayeeanzade2026psyset} evaluate emotion and personality
steering across four model families and find both controllability and
model-specific side effects on safety and truthfulness; \citet{jeong2026geometry}
report near-identical emotion geometry across twelve small models whose
\emph{behavioral} profiles diverge. We therefore claim no novelty for the
existence of emotion representations in open-weight models, nor for the
observation that similar geometry can coexist with different behavior.

\paragraph{From representation to mechanism.}
That information is decodable does not establish that the model uses it.
\citet{hewitt2019control} motivate probe controls; \citet{elazar2021amnesic} show
directly that probe accuracy need not correspond to behavioral importance.
\citet{palumbo2025axiomatic} and \citet{mueller2025mib} develop standards for
validating mechanistic interpretations and for evaluating whether methods recover
causal variables. For interventions specifically, \citet{stoehr2024scaling}
separate effectiveness from faithfulness and minimality, and
\citet{wu2026steerable} show across 17 models that apparent steering conclusions
can depend on normalization, layer and operating-point selection --- which is
precisely why we decline to rescue our causal claim with a post-hoc layer choice
(\S\ref{sec:layer}). Steering does sometimes establish genuine causal control
\citep{rimsky2024caa, arditi2024refusal}, so a null here is informative rather
than expected.

\paragraph{Latent signals that are diagnostic but not causal.}
\citet{yuan2026hidden} find hidden-state signals of reasoning errors that far
outperform verbalized confidence, yet four interventions fail to convert the
signal into control. \citet{wilhelm2026monitoring} detect reward hacking from
residual-stream activations during generation across model families. These rule
out any broad novelty claim that internal signals can be diagnostic without being
causal, or that reward hacking is detectable from activations. Our setting differs
in one respect worth stating precisely: their probes are \emph{trained on the
behavioral outcome}, whereas our direction is built from an independent emotion
corpus, semantically validated, and frozen before any behavioral analysis.

\paragraph{Novelty statement.}
We claim novelty for none of: emotion representations in LLMs, emotion steering in
open models, similar geometry with divergent behavior, diagnostic-not-causal
latent signals, activation-based reward-hacking detection, or LLM-judge
unreliability. We claim only this: \emph{a targeted validity audit of one
specific, alignment-relevant functional claim, in which several initially
compelling conclusions die under specific, named controls, with the record of
which control killed which claim released alongside.}

\section{Setup}

\paragraph{Probes.} We extract 50 emotion vectors from the Llama~3.1~70B residual
stream with PCA denoising; the pre-specified probe site is layer 53 of 80.
$V_{\text{internal}}$ for a trial is the cosine similarity between the trial's
mean residual-stream activation and a frozen emotion vector. Probes are never
refit on behavioral data, so every association below is zero-shot transfer rather
than a within-dataset classification score.

\paragraph{An important measurement caveat.} Our implementation averages
activations from token 50 to the end of the sequence, which \emph{includes the
generated response}. The activation is therefore measured concurrently with ---
not before --- the reward-hacking behavior it is compared against. We consequently
describe our tier-2 result as an \emph{association} throughout, and explicitly not
as prediction of a future choice. A pre-decision readout at the assistant-header
position, as used by \citet{sofroniew2026}, would be required to license the
stronger claim; our codebase contains such a function but the data analyzed here
were not generated with it. This is a
limitation of the present audit, and it means part of the association may reflect
the shortcut text itself rather than an antecedent state.

\paragraph{Behavior.} Reward hacking is elicited with impossible-by-construction
coding tasks, where an honest attempt must fail and a shortcut (hardcoding outputs,
faking a verifier, silently dropping a requirement) passes. A parallel sycophancy
task is excluded throughout: it produced 2 sycophantic responses in 650 trials.

\paragraph{Labels.} Responses are classified by an LLM judge over three independent
passes. Because judge choice proved consequential, we run two judges from
different families --- Qwen~2.5~72B and Claude --- and report agreement wherever
labels carry a conclusion \citep{shi2025position, zheng2023judging}.

\paragraph{Text baseline.} $V_{\text{text}}$ is an 8-dimension emotional tone
rating (1--7) of the model's chain-of-thought by the same judge, blind to steering
condition. It stands in for what a CoT monitor could observe
\citep{chen2025reasoning}.

\paragraph{Preregistration.} The tier-2 hypothesis was preregistered with a binding
decision rule. The registration discloses that it was drafted \emph{after} an
exploratory AUC of $0.900$ had been observed on an initial 40-trial subset, and it
was deliberately written so that this subset would fail. We do not claim a
pre-data-inspection standard.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Question 1: the directions are semantically valid}

Projecting the frozen probes onto EmoBank \citep{buechel2017emobank} --- 10,062
sentences with human valence--arousal--dominance ratings --- with no refitting:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
& Valence & Arousal & Dominance \\
\midrule
Cross-validated $R^2$ & \textbf{0.377} & 0.180 & 0.154 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Valence is substantially recovered, arousal and dominance weakly, with coherent
sign structure (\texttt{ecstatic} $r = +0.38$, \texttt{furious} $-0.34$,
\texttt{terrified} $-0.34$). This supports construct validity for the valence
axis. It does \emph{not} establish that the ``desperate'' vector represents a
desperation \emph{state}.

\subsection{Question 2: a concurrent behavioral association holds}

On unsteered trials only ($n = 120$, 14 hacking events), no intervention:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
Predictor & AUC & Bootstrap 95\% CI & Permutation $p$ & Mann--Whitney $p$ \\
\midrule
$V_{\text{internal}}$[desperate] & \textbf{0.832} & $[0.742,\ 0.909]$ & $< 0.001$ & $2.8\times10^{-5}$ \\
$V_{\text{internal}}$[$-$calm]   & 0.865 & --- & --- & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The association is not an artifact of judge choice: Qwen and Claude, judging
independently, agree on \textbf{120/120 trials} and identify the same 14 shortcuts.

\paragraph{But the preregistered criterion is not met.} The registration makes the
leave-one-task-out mean, not the pooled AUC, the load-bearing metric, and requires
that at least 6 of the 9 planned task variants produce outcome variance ---
explicitly to guard against pooled AUC inflated by between-task differences in
predictor scale. The five mechanistically diverse variants added to satisfy this
criterion did not yield usable labels (\S\ref{sec:judge}), so the suite still has
2 variants with outcome variance. The preregistered verdict is therefore
\textsc{insufficient-data}, and we report it as such. \textbf{We do not claim H5
as confirmed.} A strong pooled AUC that we decline to promote is, we think, the
more informative outcome to report.

\subsection{Question 3: no advantage over the text baseline at the pre-specified layer}
\label{sec:layer}

Pooled across steered and unsteered trials ($n = 992$, 87 events) the gap looks
large --- $V_{\text{internal}}$ AUC $0.836$ $[0.797, 0.872]$ vs.\ $V_{\text{text}}$
$0.647$ $[0.603, 0.690]$, $\Delta = 0.189$ $[0.141, 0.239]$ --- but that pooled
comparison inherits a task-identity confound: probes predict which task is being
solved at $95.4\%$ accuracy, and residualizing on task id drops AUC from $0.799$
to $0.674$. On the clean unsteered set, sweeping the probe layer:

\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
Layer & $V_{\text{int}}$[desp] AUC & $\Delta$ vs.\ $V_{\text{text}}$ & 95\% CI & $p$ (1-sided) \\
\midrule
13 & 0.691 & $+0.053$ & $[-0.173,\ 0.299]$ & 0.339 \\
26 & 0.450 & $-0.187$ & $[-0.493,\ 0.125]$ & 0.890 \\
39 & 0.918 & $+0.280$ & $[0.056,\ 0.479]$ & 0.008 \\
52 & 0.708 & $+0.069$ & $[-0.247,\ 0.343]$ & 0.306 \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Incremental AUC over the text baseline, unsteered trials

We therefore do not claim a faithfulness gap. Layer 39 is a hypothesis for
replication. Treating it as a result would be exactly the operating-point
selection that \citet{wu2026steerable} show can manufacture steering conclusions.
We also note that our $V_{\text{text}}$ instrument is near-degenerate --- 946
trials in the low third, \emph{zero} in the middle third, 46 in the high third,
across 14 unique rounded values --- so even the pooled gap partly measures baseline
coarseness rather than CoT unfaithfulness.

\subsection{Question 4: causal specificity is unsupported}

The decisive control: five random directions orthogonal to the emotion subspace,
steered at matched norm.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
Condition & hacks / $n$ & rate \\
\midrule
Unsteered baseline & 14 / 120 & 11.7\% \\
Emotion vector @ $\pm0.3$ & 12 / 160 & 7.5\% \\
Random directions @ $\pm0.3$ & 13 / 200 & 6.5\% \\
\midrule
\multicolumn{3}{@{}l@{}}{\emph{Emotion vs.\ random: Fisher exact $p = 0.835$}} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Steering the emotion direction does nothing that a matched arbitrary direction does
not also do. The fine-grained dose--response over $\pm0.05$ to $\pm0.5$ is flat for
desperate ($z = -1.42$, $p = 0.157$); calm reaches $p = 0.041$ uncorrected with the
wrong sign. Injecting ``feel desperate'' into the system prompt likewise fails to
separate from baseline ($17.5\%$ vs.\ $11.7\%$, Fisher $p = 0.417$).

We state the scope carefully. This does not show the representation is causally
inert in general, and it does not contradict \citet{sofroniew2026} on a different
model. It shows that \emph{at this site, at this norm, in this model, the
intervention lacks specificity} --- and that without a random-direction null we
would have reported a significant, publishable, and wrong causal effect in the
opposite direction to the original.

\subsection{Judge validity is task-dependent}
\label{sec:judge}

Our two judges agree on $120/120$ unsteered trials. On the 650-trial diverse suite
built to test cross-mechanism generalization, the same two judges agree on
$165/650$ ($25.4\%$): Qwen labels 2 responses \textsc{shortcut} and 524
\textsc{unclear}; Claude labels 194 \textsc{shortcut} and 39 \textsc{unclear}. We
therefore exclude that dataset entirely --- including a tempting negative
generalization result it appears to support --- rather than mine it. Judge
validity established on one distribution does not carry to another
\citep{shi2025position}; we recommend reporting cross-family agreement per
dataset, not per paper.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{The controls that mattered were cheap.} A matched-norm random direction,
a second judge from a different family, and a decision rule written down in advance
cost far less than the experiments they invalidated. Each removed a claim we
believed. We suggest all three as defaults rather than as robustness appendices.

\paragraph{Association and control are different products.} An association at
AUC $0.83$ might support monitoring; it does not identify an intervention target.
Because both are usually illustrated by the same object --- a named direction ---
the two narratives are easy to conflate. \citet{yuan2026hidden} report the same
dissociation in a different domain, which suggests it is not incidental.

\paragraph{Report your instrument's resolution.} Part of our apparent text-baseline
gap is baseline coarseness, not model unfaithfulness. Comparative claims should
state the resolution of the weaker instrument.

\section{Limitations}

Single model, single behavior, single extraction method; 14 events at $n = 120$.
Our probe is a response-mean readout, so tier 2 is concurrent association and not
prediction --- the single most important fix for future work. The tier-4 null bounds
the effect at one site and norm and cannot exclude effects elsewhere, and
\citet{wu2026steerable} give concrete reasons such nulls can be operating-point
artifacts. Layer 39 is a post-hoc selection from six. Whether the tier-2
association generalizes across shortcut mechanisms remains genuinely open; we
deliberately answer it from no data rather than from unreliable labels.

\section{Conclusion}

We audited one alignment-relevant mechanistic claim at four levels of evidence.
Semantic validity and a concurrent, task-local behavioral association survive; the
preregistered generalization criterion is unmet; the advantage over a text baseline
disappears at the pre-specified operating point; and the causal intervention fails a
random-direction specificity test. The methodological standards applied here are
established. What we add is a worked case in which applying them changes the
answer, together with the record of which control changed it.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Analysis scripts, judged trial records, the
preregistration with its honesty disclosure, and a claim-to-file provenance table
are released with the paper.
