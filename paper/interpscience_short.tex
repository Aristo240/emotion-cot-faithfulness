\title{A Validity Audit of Emotion Probes in Llama 3.1 70B}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\citet{sofroniew2026} report that emotion concepts in Claude Sonnet 4.5 are
functional, in that steering a ``desperate'' direction increases reward hacking. We
test each claim that interpretation rests on in Llama~3.1~70B. Four controls change
the conclusion.
(i)~\emph{Response length.} The desperate projection reaches AUC $0.832$ against
reward hacking, but it correlates with response length at $\rho = 0.61$.
Residualized on length it falls to $0.592$ and adds no detectable information
to a length-only model ($\chi^2(1) = 3.3$, $p = 0.069$, and $p = 0.44$ once
corrected across all 50 directions).
(ii)~\emph{Multiplicity.} A family-wise-corrected sweep of all 50 directions leaves
18 significant. Those 18 are largely one shared axis rather than 18 findings, with $78.8\%$ of
the variance in the first principal component, and it is not the axis we
registered. The count is soft. Eleven survive every nuisance model we tried
and only 8 recur under a magnitude-removed readout.
(iii)~\emph{Specificity.} We detect no increase in reward hacking from
steering the registered direction at $+0.3$, at $1/39$ against $14/120$
unsteered, though the interval is wide. Steering does shift the targeted
projection by $0.48$ SD, measured on a clean re-encoding of the output, which
makes a wholly inert intervention less likely without showing that the named
state was induced.
(iv)~\emph{Instrument resolution.} The chain-of-thought text baseline cannot support
the comparison it was used for. $55\%$ of its ROC pairs are ties, so the pooled $0.19$ AUC gap cannot separate
a genuine hidden-versus-text dissociation from an instrument too coarse to
register one.
None of these controls is novel. The contribution is one worked case in which each
of them changes the answer, with every number regenerated by a released script.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A common inferential shortcut runs as follows. We found an interpretable
direction, it tracks a behavior we care about, so it is a mechanism and
steering it is a lever. Each link is a separate empirical claim, and such claims routinely outrun
their causal evidence \citep{joshi2026causality}.

\citet{sofroniew2026} offer a well-specified target. Emotion concepts in Claude
Sonnet 4.5 causally drive alignment-relevant behavior, with a ``desperate''
direction substantially increasing reward hacking, sometimes with no visible trace
in output text. We ask what survives in Llama~3.1~70B. This is a conceptual, not
direct, replication, because the model, the extraction procedure and the steering
norm all differ. A null here cannot separate model-specific functionality from implementation
differences. Of the seven claims below, one is supported, one has exploratory support, one is not supported, and four are unresolved for different reasons
(Table~\ref{tab:summary}).

\section{Related work and novelty statement}

\paragraph{Emotion representations, and what they do not establish.}
Emotion concepts are recoverable from LLM hidden states, transfer across datasets,
are steerable across model families, and have near-identical geometry across models
whose behavior diverges \citep{maheswaran2026unified, banayeeanzade2026psyset,
jeong2026geometry}. Decodability is not use, however
\citep{hewitt2019control, elazar2021amnesic}, and standards for validating
interpretations and interventions exist \citep{palumbo2025axiomatic, mueller2025mib,
stoehr2024scaling}. \citet{wu2026steerable} show across 17 models that steering
conclusions can turn on normalization, layer and operating point, which is why we
decline a post-hoc layer choice (\S\ref{sec:causal}). Steering does sometimes
establish real control \citep{rimsky2024caa, arditi2024refusal}, so a null is
informative rather than expected. \citet{yuan2026hidden} report hidden-state error signals that resist four
interventions, the closest prior case of a signal that is diagnostic but not
causal.
\citet{wilhelm2026monitoring} detect reward hacking from residual-stream activations
during generation.

\paragraph{Novelty statement.}
That work pre-empts novelty for emotion representations, steering,
diagnostic-not-causal signals, reward-hacking detection, and every control we
apply. What we add is empirical. We audit one alignment-relevant functional claim end
to end and show that each evidential layer gives a different answer. Our
probes also come from an independent emotion corpus and are frozen before any
behavioral analysis, rather than trained on the outcome.

\section{Setup}

\paragraph{Probes.} We extract 50 emotion vectors from the Llama~3.1~70B residual
stream with PCA denoising, at the pre-specified site of layer 53 of 80.
$V_{\text{int}}$ projects a trial's mean activation onto a frozen unit-norm emotion
direction. The vectors are never refit on behavioral data. Our preregistration
describes this quantity as a \emph{cosine similarity} and it is not. The code
normalizes by the emotion vector's norm but not by the activation's, so the value is
$\lVert \bar a \rVert \cos\theta$. \S\ref{sec:dirs} and Appendix~\ref{app:readout} test what difference this
makes.

\paragraph{Measurement caveat.} We average activations from token 50
to end-of-sequence, so the readout window \emph{includes the generated response}.
The probe is measured at the same time as the behavior and is entangled with how
much text was produced. This motivates \S\ref{sec:len}, and we report associations throughout rather
than prediction.

\paragraph{Registration.} Our analysis plan was written after an exploratory AUC of $0.900$, so
``registered'' below marks planned rather than pre-data analyses. Trials
labelled \textsc{unclear} are dropped, which is why one steering cell has 39
trials rather than 40. \paragraph{Behavior and labels.} Reward hacking is
elicited with a single coding task, \texttt{fast\_sum}, in four impossible-by-construction variants where an
honest attempt must fail. A near-eventless sycophancy task is excluded throughout.
Responses are labeled by an LLM judge over three passes.
\texttt{scripts/paper\_numbers.py} regenerates every number here, and a second
script gates this manuscript against it with 292 assertions.

\paragraph{Cross-judge agreement and abstention.} Agreement bounds reliability rather than accuracy, and neither judge is validated
against human labels. On the 120 unsteered trials used below, Qwen~2.5~72B and
Claude agree \textbf{120/120} with no abstentions. On a 650-trial suite of five
other shortcut mechanisms the primary judge abstains (\textsc{unclear}) on $80.6\%$
of trials. Where both commit they never contradict each other (\textbf{0/126},
$95\%$ upper bound $2.4\%$). What fails to transfer is
willingness to label rather than agreement. The abstention is also not independent
of the outcome, running $99\%$ on trials Claude calls \textsc{shortcut} against
$73\%$ elsewhere (Fisher $p = 4\times10^{-19}$). We exclude the suite entirely,
including a negative generalization result it appears to support, because analyzing
what remains would condition on the outcome
\citep{shi2025position, zheng2023judging}.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Semantic validity against human ratings}
\label{sec:sem}

We project the frozen probes onto EmoBank \citep{buechel2017emobank}, 10,062
human-rated sentences. The emotion vectors are frozen, but a ridge readout from the
50 projections to the valence, arousal and dominance ratings is fitted inside each
cross-validation fold, so the reported $R^2$ is out-of-fold rather than zero-shot.
Cross-validated $R^2$ is $\mathbf{0.377}$ for valence, $0.180$ for arousal and
$0.154$ for dominance. Unigram TF-IDF under the same folds and the same estimator
reaches $0.219$, $0.092$ and $0.063$. The probe clears the trivial text baseline. This supports construct validity
for an affect-related axis, not the label on any individual vector.

\subsection{The preregistered association is not separable from response
length}
\label{sec:len}

On unsteered trials ($n = 120$, 14 events) the preregistered predictor performs
well, and so does character count. $V_{\text{int}}$[desperate] reaches AUC
$\mathbf{0.832}$ (bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.745, 0.908]$) and \texttt{len(response)}
reaches $\mathbf{0.888}$ $[0.787, 0.967]$.

Length does not \emph{beat} the probe (paired bootstrap $\Delta$AUC $+0.057$,
$[-0.080, +0.183]$). What we cannot show is that the probe adds to it.
$\rho(V_{\text{int}}[\text{desperate}], \text{length}) = 0.606$
($p = 2.2\times10^{-13}$). Adding the probe to a length-only logistic model gives
$\chi^2(1) = 3.31$, $p = 0.069$. Residualizing on length leaves AUC $= \mathbf{0.592}$ $[0.412, 0.756]$, a
descriptive interval wide enough to admit a real effect. The direction also
ranks only 8th of 50 by raw AUC. The readout averages over the generated response, so response length changes
which token activations enter the average and with what weight. That is a
direct route for length-associated features to reach the probe, and it would
produce this pattern with no emotional content at all. Length could instead mediate a genuine effect, in
which case residualizing over-adjusts. This design cannot separate the two.
What it does show is that the preregistered association is not identifiable
independently of response length.

\subsection{A corrected sweep over all 50 directions}
\label{sec:dirs}

Length residualization does not empty the emotion subspace. We repeat the
analysis for all 50 directions with the nested likelihood-ratio test,
correcting selection by max-$T$ over the largest of the 50 statistics, so
significance is not an artifact of choosing a direction after seeing the data.

Because length alone predicts the outcome at AUC $0.888$, the null preserves
the outcome--length relationship rather than permuting labels freely, and
because 14 events allow quasi-separation the estimates are ridge-penalized.
There is no complete separation. Calibration comes from the resampling
distribution rather than a nominal $\chi^2_1$ reference. Neither choice drives
the result, since both nulls give the same 18 directions and sweeping
$\lambda$ moves the count only between 16 and 18 with \texttt{desperate} null
throughout ($p \ge 0.38$). Appendix~\ref{app:maxt} has the procedure.

Eighteen of 50 directions are significant at $\alpha = .05$, and that count is
the fragile part. Monte Carlo noise alone moves it to 17 at $B = 2000$.
Changing the nuisance model moves it further, to 14 under a cubic-plus-knots
length basis and to 11 under fixed effects for the four task variants, whose
event rates are uneven at $9/30$ against $0/30$. Eleven directions clear all
three nuisance models. They are the low-arousal negative core plus \texttt{compassionate} and
\texttt{proud}, and what drops out is the positive-affect and high-arousal
periphery. \texttt{desperate} is null in all of them ($p \ge 0.43$).

\paragraph{The survivors are dominated by a shared component.} Their
trial-level projections are heavily intercorrelated, with mean $|r| = 0.76$,
$78.8\%$ of the variance in the first principal component, and a participation
ratio of $1.58$. Low-arousal negative
affect sits at one pole and positive affect together with \texttt{compassionate}
at the other, with $\beta$ signs reversing accordingly. One component detected many times fits this better than 18 findings. We cannot
say from these numbers whether that component is affective or the shared
activation-magnitude factor of \S3, since the two are not separated here.

\paragraph{The axis survives a direction-only readout, but its composition does not.}
Because the probe scales with activation magnitude (\S3), we repeated the sweep on
each trial's 50-vector normalized to unit length, which cancels the shared
$\lVert \bar a \rVert$ factor exactly. An association remains, but only 8 of the 18
survivors are shared, so whether the axis is best called valence or negative affect
turns on a measurement choice our preregistration mis-specified.
Appendix~\ref{app:readout} gives the numbers.

This exploratory association survives family-wise correction across the 50
tested directions, and it is \emph{not the hypothesis we registered}. The
correction handles selection among directions, not the fact that the sweep was
run after the registered direction failed. We do not assert that
\texttt{bored} measures boredom. The narrow claim is that some component of
this subspace carries information about reward hacking that length does not.

\subsection{Resolution limits of the text baseline}
\label{sec:vtext}

We had intended to compare $V_{\text{int}}$ against a judge-rated
chain-of-thought tone composite, $V_{\text{text}}$. On the pooled set ($n =
992$, 87 events) $V_{\text{text}}$ holds one modal value for $74.0\%$ of
trials, so $\mathbf{55.2\%}$ of positive--negative ROC pairs are ties
contributing $0.5$ each.

$V_{\text{text}}$ reaches AUC $0.647$ overall against $V_{\text{int}}$'s
$0.837$, but $\mathbf{0.836}$ against $0.890$ on the 258 trials (22 events)
where it is not at its mode. That subset is selected on the predictor's own values, so the gaps do not
decompose cleanly. What the tie structure establishes is that the pooled gap
cannot separate a real hidden-versus-text dissociation from an instrument too
coarse to register one. We withdraw any faithfulness-gap claim, including a quadrant
analysis an earlier draft built on this near-constant variable.

The claim under audit is directional, so the arms have to be read by sign.
Pooling $+0.3$ with $-0.3$ would let an effect in one direction cancel the
other, and the pooled emotion arm also contains \texttt{calm}. That pooled
contrast is a specificity test and nothing more.
Taken directionally, we detect no increase from $+0.3$ \emph{desperate}, at
$2.6\%$ against $11.7\%$ unsteered (Fisher $p = 0.12$), so the point estimate
runs opposite to the registered prediction rather than falling short of it.
The random arm is not balanced with it across task variants, whose base rates
range from $0/30$ to $9/30$, so we do not treat the random comparison as a
clean specificity test (Appendix~\ref{app:steering}). The dose--response over $\pm0.05$ to $\pm0.5$ is flat ($z = -1.42$, $p =
0.157$), and a ``feel desperate'' system prompt also fails (Fisher $p =
0.417$).

\paragraph{The intervention moved the targeted coordinate.} A behavioral null is ambiguous unless the manipulation worked. The steering
hooks are removed before extraction, so the projection is read from a clean
re-encoding of the generated text and the shift is not mechanically induced by
adding the vector.
Steering \emph{desperate} moves the desperate projection monotonically with
signed strength, and the shift at $+0.3$ is $0.48$ SD of the unsteered
projection (Mann--Whitney $p = 0.025$). For scale, hacking and non-hacking
trials differ by $1.12$ SD on the same readout, though that separation is
itself length-entangled, so the ratio bounds the manipulation's size and not
its behavioral relevance. This shows the
intervention altered the targeted coordinate. It does not show that it induced
the state the label names.

\paragraph{What this null does and does not exclude.} The directional contrast
is weak, with one event in 39 trials and an interval $[0.04, 2.17]$ that
excludes very little, so this is an undetected effect rather than a
demonstrated absence. The ${\sim}14\times$ \citet{sofroniew2026} report is not
portable to our $11.7\%$ base rate, though an absolute increase of that size
would have been visible. Appendix~\ref{app:steering} gives the intervals and
the arm imbalance.

\paragraph{Why no layer rescues the registered direction.}
A null at one layer invites the objection that we picked the wrong site, and
our sweep appeared to supply it. Raw AUC for $V_{\text{int}}$[desperate] peaks
at layer 39, at $0.918$ on an 80-trial subset with 7 events, against $0.677$
at the steered layer. Applying \S\ref{sec:len}'s control removes every layer's
advantage. \textbf{No evaluated layer gives
the preregistered direction length-independent signal detectable at $n = 80$}, so we
have no observational reason to move the intervention elsewhere. Steering at a post-hoc site chosen on a confounded statistic is the failure
mode \citet{wu2026steerable} describe. We steered only at the registered
layer. Appendix~\ref{app:layers} gives the
per-layer numbers and the geometry across layers.

\subsection{The preregistered generalization criterion remains unmet}
\label{sec:prereg}

The registration makes the leave-one-task-out mean load-bearing rather than
pooled AUC, and requires $\ge 6$ of 9 task variants to produce outcome
variance. The five variants added to satisfy it are the ones \S3 excludes,
because the primary judge labels one of the five mechanisms and abstains on
$\ge 98\%$ of the other four. The verdict remains \textsc{insufficient-data}.


\section{Limitations}

This is a single model, one task family in four variants, and 14 events, so the
inference is about these rollouts rather than reward hacking in general. The readout window includes the response, so \S\ref{sec:len} cannot separate
measurement artifact from mediation. Max-$T$ controls selection among
directions but not the possibility that the survivors index a further
unmeasured response property, and the count is nuisance-model-sensitive. The
causal null is bounded at one steering norm and one layer.
No layer in our sweep offers a length-independent version of the registered
direction, but other token positions, extraction methods and norms remain
open, and we never steered the directions that \emph{do} survive. Whether any
association generalizes across mechanisms is open, and we answer it from no
data rather than from a subset our judge selected.

\section{Discussion}

\emph{Trivial-baseline controls belong beside random-direction controls.} The field requires a steering effect to beat a random direction but rarely
requires a probe to beat a trivial output feature. Here a character count
matched one. \emph{Report the resolution of the instrument you
compare against.} A predictor constant for $74\%$ of trials still yields an AUC, but not one
that can carry a faithfulness reading, so report tie fractions beside AUCs. \emph{Correcting for selection can support
a result as well as remove one.} The same max-$T$ sweep that forbids the post-hoc
layer choice in \S\ref{sec:causal} licenses the one exploratory association we
report. Each step tests a different inferential link, and passing one says nothing
about the next.
