\title{A Validity Audit of Emotion Probes in Llama 3.1 70B}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\citet{sofroniew2026} report that emotion concepts in Claude Sonnet 4.5 are
functional: steering a ``desperate'' direction increases reward hacking. We test
each claim that interpretation rests on in Llama~3.1~70B. Four controls change the
conclusion.
(i)~\emph{Response length.} The desperate projection reaches AUC $0.832$ but
correlates with response length at $\rho = 0.61$; residualized it
falls to $0.592$, adding nothing to a length-only model ($\chi^2(1) = 3.3$,
family-wise $p = 0.44$).
(ii)~\emph{Multiplicity.} A family-wise-corrected sweep of all 50 directions leaves
18 significant --- one axis, not 18 findings ($78.8\%$ of variance in the first
principal component), and not the one we registered. The count is soft: 11 survive
every nuisance model we tried and only 8 recur under a magnitude-removed readout, so
the axis is real and its membership is not.
(iii)~\emph{Specificity.} Emotion steering is indistinguishable from matched-norm
random directions ($7.5\%$ vs.\ $6.5\%$; risk ratio $1.15$, $95\%$ CI
$[0.54, 2.46]$), which excludes effects above $\sim\!2.4\times$.
(iv)~\emph{Instrument resolution.} The chain-of-thought text baseline cannot support
the comparison: $55\%$ of its ROC pairs are ties, and on trials where it resolves at
all it trails the probe by $0.05$, not the $0.19$ the pooled AUCs suggest.
None of these controls is novel; the contribution is a case in which each one
changes the answer, with every number regenerated by a released script.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A common inferential shortcut runs: we found an interpretable direction; it tracks a
behavior we care about; therefore it is a mechanism, and steering it is a lever. Each
link is a separate empirical claim, and such claims routinely outrun their causal
evidence \citep{joshi2026causality}.

\citet{sofroniew2026} offer a well-specified target: emotion concepts in Claude
Sonnet 4.5 that causally drive alignment-relevant behavior, with a ``desperate''
direction substantially increasing reward hacking, sometimes with no visible trace
in output text. We ask what survives transfer to Llama~3.1~70B. The answer is not
uniformly negative: of the seven claims below, two are supported, three are not, and
two cannot be settled with our data (Table~\ref{tab:summary}).

\section{Related work and novelty statement}

\paragraph{Emotion representations, and what they do not establish.}
Emotion concepts are recoverable from LLM hidden states, transfer across datasets,
are steerable across model families, and have near-identical geometry across models
whose behavior diverges \citep{maheswaran2026unified, banayeeanzade2026psyset,
jeong2026geometry}. Decodability is not use, however
\citep{hewitt2019control, elazar2021amnesic}, and standards for validating
interpretations and interventions exist \citep{palumbo2025axiomatic, mueller2025mib,
stoehr2024scaling}. \citet{wu2026steerable} show across 17 models that steering
conclusions can turn on normalization, layer and operating point, which is why we
decline a post-hoc layer choice (\S\ref{sec:causal}); steering does sometimes
establish real control \citep{rimsky2024caa, arditi2024refusal}, so a null is
informative rather than expected. Closest to us, \citet{yuan2026hidden} and
\citet{wilhelm2026monitoring} find hidden-state signals that are diagnostic but not
controllable.

\paragraph{Novelty statement.}
That work pre-empts novelty for emotion representations, emotion steering,
diagnostic-not-causal signals, activation-based reward-hacking detection, and every
control we apply. One methodological difference is worth
stating: our probes come from an independent emotion corpus, frozen before any
behavioral analysis rather than trained on the outcome.

\section{Setup}

\paragraph{Probes.} 50 emotion vectors extracted from the Llama~3.1~70B residual
stream with PCA denoising; pre-specified site layer 53 of 80. $V_{\text{int}}$
projects a trial's mean activation onto a frozen unit-norm emotion direction, never
refit on behavioral data.
Our preregistration describes this quantity as a \emph{cosine similarity}; it is
not. The code
normalizes by the emotion vector's norm but not the activation's, so the value is
$\lVert \bar a \rVert \cos\theta$, and the true cosine is unrecoverable because
$\lVert \bar a \rVert$ was never stored. \S\ref{sec:dirs} tests what difference this
makes.

\paragraph{Measurement caveat (consequential).} We average activations from token 50
to end-of-sequence, so the readout window \emph{includes the generated response}:
the probe is measured concurrently with the behavior and entangled with how much
text was produced. Hence \S\ref{sec:len}; we report associations throughout, never
prediction.

\paragraph{Behavior and labels.} Reward hacking is elicited with a single coding
task, \texttt{fast\_sum}, in four impossible-by-construction variants where an honest
attempt must fail; a near-eventless sycophancy task is excluded throughout. Responses are labeled by an
LLM judge over three passes. \texttt{scripts/paper\_numbers.py} regenerates every
number here, and a second script gates this manuscript against it
with 263 assertions.

\paragraph{Judge validity.} Checked per dataset. On the 120 unsteered trials used
below, Qwen~2.5~72B and Claude agree \textbf{120/120} with no abstentions. On a
650-trial suite of five other shortcut mechanisms the primary judge abstains
(\textsc{unclear}) on $80.6\%$, and where both judges commit they never contradict
each other (\textbf{0/126}; $95\%$ upper bound $2.4\%$). What fails to transfer is
willingness to label, not agreement --- and the abstention is not independent of the
outcome: $99\%$ on trials Claude calls \textsc{shortcut} against $73\%$ elsewhere
(Fisher $p = 4\times10^{-19}$). We exclude the suite entirely, including a negative
generalization result it appears to support: analyzing what remains would condition
on the outcome \citep{shi2025position, zheng2023judging}.


\section{Results}

\subsection{Semantic validity against human ratings}
\label{sec:sem}

Projecting the frozen probes onto EmoBank \citep{buechel2017emobank} --- 10,062
human-rated sentences, no refitting --- gives cross-validated $R^2$ of
$\mathbf{0.377}$ (valence), $0.180$ (arousal), $0.154$ (dominance), against
$0.219$/$0.092$/$0.063$ for unigram TF-IDF on the same sentences and folds. The
probe clears the trivial baseline we ask of everyone else, supporting construct
validity for a valence axis --- not for any individual vector's label.

\subsection{Response length accounts for the preregistered association}
\label{sec:len}

On unsteered trials ($n = 120$, 14 events) the preregistered predictor performs
well --- and so does character count. $V_{\text{int}}$[desperate] reaches AUC
$\mathbf{0.832}$ (bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.745, 0.908]$); \texttt{len(response)}
reaches $\mathbf{0.888}$ $[0.787, 0.967]$.

Length does not \emph{beat} the probe (paired bootstrap $\Delta$AUC $+0.057$
$[-0.080, +0.183]$); the problem is that the probe carries nothing else:
$\rho(V_{\text{int}}[\text{desperate}], \text{length}) = 0.606$
($p = 2.2\times10^{-13}$); adding the probe to a length-only logistic model gives
$\chi^2(1) = 3.31$, $p = 0.069$; and residualizing on length leaves AUC
$= \mathbf{0.592}$ $[0.412, 0.756]$, a descriptive interval rather than a test. Nor
is the direction distinctive before that correction: it ranks 8th of 50 by raw AUC,
behind
\texttt{bored} at $0.985$. We read this as a measurement artifact, not a substantive
null: the readout window spans the response, so a longer response mechanically
changes the average the probe projects. This design cannot separate that from length
\emph{mediating} a genuine effect, in which case residualizing over-adjusts; either
way the preregistered claim is not supported as stated.

\subsection{A corrected sweep over all 50 directions}
\label{sec:dirs}

Length residualization does not empty the emotion subspace. We repeat the analysis
for all 50 directions with the nested likelihood-ratio test --- does the direction
add to a model that already contains length? --- correcting for selection by
max-$T$, whose reference distribution is that of the \emph{largest} of the 50
statistics, so significance survives post-hoc choice.

Two design choices matter, and neither drives the result. \emph{The null.} A free
label permutation tests the \emph{marginal} hypothesis $y \perp \text{direction}$,
but length alone predicts the outcome at AUC $0.888$, so free permutation destroys
the dependence the statistic conditions on. We therefore also draw from a
\emph{conditional} null --- $y^* \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\hat p)$, $\hat p$ from an
unpenalized length-only fit, $B = 10{,}000$ --- and report it as the principled
choice, though both nulls give the same 18 directions with per-direction
$p$ within $0.02$. (At $B = 2000$ it looked stricter; Monte Carlo noise.)
\emph{The penalty.} With 14 events an unpenalized fit can quasi-separate --- at
$\lambda = 0$, 13 fits fail to converge --- so ridge-penalized estimates (intercept
unpenalized) are primary; there is no complete separation, and sweeping
$\lambda \in \{0, 0.5, 1, 2\}$ moves the count only between 16 and 18, leaving
\texttt{desperate} null throughout ($p \le 0.50$).

Eighteen of 50 directions are significant at $\alpha = .05$, and that count is the
fragile part: 17 at $B = 2000$, 14 when linear length is replaced by a
cubic-plus-knots basis, and 11 under fixed effects for the four task variants, whose
event rates are uneven ($9/30$ against $0/30$). Eleven clear all three models --- the
low-arousal negative core plus \texttt{compassionate} and
\texttt{proud}; what drops out is the positive-affect and high-arousal periphery.
\texttt{desperate} is null in all of them ($p \ge 0.43$).

\paragraph{The survivors are one axis, not eighteen findings.} They are heavily
intercorrelated (mean $|r| = 0.76$; first principal component $78.8\%$ of variance;
participation ratio $1.58$) --- a single axis detected many times, with low-arousal
negative affect at one pole and positive affect plus \texttt{compassionate} at the
other, $\beta$ signs reversing accordingly.

\paragraph{The axis survives a direction-only readout; its composition does not.}
Because the probe scales with activation magnitude (\S3), we repeated the sweep on
each trial's 50-vector normalized to unit length, cancelling the shared
$\lVert \bar a \rVert$ factor exactly. An association remains --- 15 of 50 directions
significant, \texttt{desperate} more clearly null ($\chi^2 = 0.25$, $p = 1.000$) ---
but only 8 of the 18 survivors are shared, all but \texttt{grateful} of them
low-arousal negative. Whether the axis is best called valence or negative affect
therefore turns on a measurement choice our preregistration mis-specified.
Normalization also leaves the length dependence intact
($\rho = 0.61 \rightarrow 0.66$), so magnitude and length are distinct nuisances.

This is a genuine, selection-corrected finding, and \emph{not the hypothesis we
registered}. We do not assert that \texttt{bored} measures boredom: it may index an
unmeasured response property, exactly as \texttt{desperate} indexed length. The
narrow claim is that some axis in the emotion subspace carries information about
shortcut-taking that length does not.

\subsection{Resolution limits of the text baseline}
\label{sec:vtext}

We had intended to compare $V_{\text{int}}$ against a judge-rated chain-of-thought
tone composite, $V_{\text{text}}$; that comparison is not evaluable here. On the
pooled set ($n = 992$, 87 events) $V_{\text{text}}$ holds one modal value for
$74.0\%$ of trials --- its \texttt{frustration} component is 1.0 in 985/992 --- so
$43{,}485$ of $78{,}735$ positive--negative ROC pairs
($\mathbf{55.2\%}$) are ties contributing $0.5$ each.

$V_{\text{text}}$ therefore reaches AUC $0.647$ overall against $V_{\text{int}}$'s
$0.837$, but $\mathbf{0.836}$ on the 258 trials (22 events) where it is not at its
mode --- against $0.890$ for $V_{\text{int}}$ on those same trials. On the trials it
can resolve it trails the probe by $0.054$, not $0.190$: most of the gap is tie
compression in a non-responsive instrument, not chain-of-thought unfaithfulness. We
withdraw any faithfulness-gap claim, including a quadrant analysis an earlier draft
built on this near-constant variable.

\subsection{Causal specificity against a matched-norm random null}
\label{sec:causal}

\begin{tabular}{@{}lccl@{}}

Steering the emotion direction does nothing a matched arbitrary direction does not
also do. Neither arm differs from baseline, so this is not capability degradation
(mean response lengths 1402 / 1324 / 1456 characters); the dose--response over
$\pm0.05$ to $\pm0.5$ is flat ($z = -1.42$, $p = 0.157$); and a ``feel desperate''
system prompt also fails to separate from baseline ($17.5\%$ vs.\ $11.7\%$, Fisher
$p = 0.417$). Pooling the random arm masks no heterogeneity: its five directions
have individual rates $0.025$--$0.075$.

\paragraph{What this null does and does not exclude.} The emotion arm's risk ratio
against the random arm is $1.15$, $95\%$ CI $[0.54, \mathbf{2.46}]$, so the design
rules out anything above about $2.4\times$ and leaves smaller effects open; the
$80\%$-power MDE, $2.42\times$, agrees. \citet{sofroniew2026} report
$\sim\!14\times$, far outside that bound --- though on a base rate we cannot match
($14\times$ is unattainable from our $11.7\%$), so the two compare on the relative
scale only.

\paragraph{Why no layer rescues the registered direction.}
A null at one layer invites the objection that we picked the wrong site, and our
sweep appeared to supply it: on the 80-trial subset for which activations were
re-extracted (7 events), raw AUC for $V_{\text{int}}$[desperate] ranges from
$0.450$ (layer 26) to $\mathbf{0.918}$ (layer 39), against $0.677$ at the steered
layer. Applying \S\ref{sec:len}'s control removes every layer's advantage: length in tokens
--- the units the probe averages over --- alone reaches AUC $0.912$.

Layer 39's apparent superiority is the same length artifact, and its residualized
CI spans chance. \textbf{No layer in the sweep gives the preregistered direction
length-independent signal detectable at $n = 80$}, so we have no operating point at
which steering it would test our registered hypothesis --- and steering at a post-hoc site chosen on a
confounded statistic is the failure mode \citet{wu2026steerable} describe.

Layers 13 and 26 are significantly \emph{anti}-predictive after residualization, but
that is different geometry, not a sign flip: their full 50-direction
residualized-AUC profiles are nearly uncorrelated with layer 53's ($\rho = 0.24$
both) where layers 39/52/53/65 form a tightly correlated block (Spearman
$0.61$--$0.99$). The behaviorally
associated axis is a late-layer phenomenon, and layer 39 correlates with the steered
layer 53 at $\rho = 0.82$, so the two sites largely measure the same thing.

\paragraph{Scope.} This is a conceptual, not direct, replication --- different model,
extraction and steering norms than \citet{sofroniew2026} --- so the claim is only
that at this norm, in this model, and at every layer we evaluate, steering lacks
specificity.

\subsection{The preregistered generalization criterion remains unmet}
\label{sec:prereg}

The registration makes the leave-one-task-out mean load-bearing, not pooled AUC, and
requires $\ge 6$ of 9 task variants to produce outcome variance --- a guard against
between-task scale differences inflating pooled AUC. The five variants added to satisfy it are the ones
\S3 excludes: the primary judge labels one of the five mechanisms and abstains on
$\ge 98\%$ of the other four, so the verdict remains \textsc{insufficient-data}. The registration itself discloses being drafted after an
exploratory AUC of $0.900$; we claim no pre-data standard.

\section{Limitations}

Single model, one task family in four variants, 14 events. The probe's readout window
includes the response, so \S\ref{sec:len} cannot separate measurement artifact from
mediation. Max-$T$ controls selection for the post-hoc \S\ref{sec:dirs} cluster, but
not the possibility that those directions index a further unmeasured response
property, and the survivor count is nuisance-model-sensitive. The causal null is
bounded at one steering norm: no layer in our sweep offers a length-independent
version of the registered direction, but other token positions, extraction methods
and norms remain open, and we never steered the directions that \emph{do} survive
\S\ref{sec:dirs}. Whether any association generalizes across mechanisms is genuinely
open: we answer that from no data rather than from a subset our judge selected.

\section{Discussion}

\emph{Trivial-baseline controls belong beside random-direction controls.} The field
requires a steering effect to beat a random direction, but rarely requires a probe
to beat a trivial feature of the output; here a character count accounted for the
association at every layer. \emph{Report the resolution of the instrument you
compare against.} A predictor constant for $74\%$ of trials gives no comparison at all, so
tie fractions belong beside AUCs. \emph{Correcting for selection can
support a result as well as remove one.} The same max-$T$ sweep that forbids the
post-hoc layer choice in \S\ref{sec:causal} licenses the one positive association we
report. None is new; the contribution is the case in which each changes the
answer.
