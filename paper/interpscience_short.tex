\title{A Validity Audit of Emotion Probes in Llama 3.1 70B}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\citet{sofroniew2026} report that emotion concepts in Claude Sonnet 4.5 are
functional, in that steering a ``desperate'' direction increases reward hacking. We
test each claim that interpretation rests on in Llama~3.1~70B. Four controls change
the conclusion.
(i)~\emph{Response length.} The desperate projection reaches AUC $0.832$ against
reward hacking, but it correlates with response length at $\rho = 0.61$.
Residualized on length it falls to $0.592$ and adds nothing to a length-only model
($\chi^2(1) = 3.3$, family-wise $p = 0.44$).
(ii)~\emph{Multiplicity.} A family-wise-corrected sweep of all 50 directions leaves
18 significant. Those 18 are largely one shared axis rather than 18 findings, with $78.8\%$ of
the variance in the first principal component, and it is not the axis we
registered. The count is soft. Eleven survive every nuisance model we tried
and only 8 recur under a magnitude-removed readout.
(iii)~\emph{Specificity.} Emotion steering is indistinguishable from matched-norm
random directions ($7.5\%$ vs.\ $6.5\%$, risk ratio $1.15$, $95\%$ CI
$[0.54, 2.46]$), which rules out effects above about $2.4\times$.
(iv)~\emph{Instrument resolution.} The chain-of-thought text baseline cannot support
the comparison it was used for. $55\%$ of its ROC pairs are ties, and where it resolves at all it trails the
probe by $0.05$ rather than the $0.19$ the pooled AUCs suggest.
None of these controls is novel. The contribution is one worked case in which each
of them changes the answer, with every number regenerated by a released script.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A common inferential shortcut runs as follows. We found an interpretable
direction, it tracks a behavior we care about, so it is a mechanism and
steering it is a lever. Each link is a separate empirical claim, and such claims routinely outrun
their causal evidence \citep{joshi2026causality}.

\citet{sofroniew2026} offer a well-specified target. Emotion concepts in Claude
Sonnet 4.5 causally drive alignment-relevant behavior, with a ``desperate''
direction substantially increasing reward hacking, sometimes with no visible trace
in output text. We ask what survives in Llama~3.1~70B. This is a conceptual, not
direct, replication, because the model, the extraction procedure and the steering
norm all differ. A null here cannot separate model-specific functionality from implementation
differences. Of the seven claims below, one is supported, one has exploratory support, three are not
supported, and two cannot be settled with our data (Table~\ref{tab:summary}).

\section{Related work and novelty statement}

\paragraph{Emotion representations, and what they do not establish.}
Emotion concepts are recoverable from LLM hidden states, transfer across datasets,
are steerable across model families, and have near-identical geometry across models
whose behavior diverges \citep{maheswaran2026unified, banayeeanzade2026psyset,
jeong2026geometry}. Decodability is not use, however
\citep{hewitt2019control, elazar2021amnesic}, and standards for validating
interpretations and interventions exist \citep{palumbo2025axiomatic, mueller2025mib,
stoehr2024scaling}. \citet{wu2026steerable} show across 17 models that steering
conclusions can turn on normalization, layer and operating point, which is why we
decline a post-hoc layer choice (\S\ref{sec:causal}). Steering does sometimes
establish real control \citep{rimsky2024caa, arditi2024refusal}, so a null is
informative rather than expected. \citet{yuan2026hidden} report hidden-state error signals that resist four
interventions, the closest prior case of a signal that is diagnostic but not
causal.
\citet{wilhelm2026monitoring} detect reward hacking from residual-stream activations
during generation.

\paragraph{Novelty statement.}
That work pre-empts novelty for emotion representations, emotion steering,
diagnostic-not-causal signals, activation-based reward-hacking detection, and every
control we apply. What we add is empirical rather than methodological. We audit one alignment-relevant functional claim end to end and show that each
evidential layer gives a different answer. One methodological difference is worth stating. Our probes come from an
independent emotion corpus and are frozen before any behavioral analysis.

\section{Setup}

\paragraph{Probes.} We extract 50 emotion vectors from the Llama~3.1~70B residual
stream with PCA denoising, at the pre-specified site of layer 53 of 80.
$V_{\text{int}}$ projects a trial's mean activation onto a frozen unit-norm emotion
direction. The vectors are never refit on behavioral data. Our preregistration
describes this quantity as a \emph{cosine similarity} and it is not. The code
normalizes by the emotion vector's norm but not by the activation's, so the value is
$\lVert \bar a \rVert \cos\theta$. The true cosine is unrecoverable because
$\lVert \bar a \rVert$ was never stored. \S\ref{sec:dirs} tests what difference this
makes.

\paragraph{Measurement caveat.} We average activations from token 50
to end-of-sequence, so the readout window \emph{includes the generated response}.
The probe is measured at the same time as the behavior and is entangled with how
much text was produced. This motivates \S\ref{sec:len}, and we report associations throughout rather
than prediction.

\paragraph{Behavior and labels.} Reward hacking is elicited with a single coding
task, \texttt{fast\_sum}, in four impossible-by-construction variants where an
honest attempt must fail. A near-eventless sycophancy task is excluded throughout.
Responses are labeled by an LLM judge over three passes.
\texttt{scripts/paper\_numbers.py} regenerates every number here, and a second
script gates this manuscript against it with 267 assertions.

\paragraph{Cross-judge agreement and abstention.} Agreement bounds reliability rather than accuracy, and neither judge is validated
against human labels. On the 120 unsteered trials used below, Qwen~2.5~72B and
Claude agree \textbf{120/120} with no abstentions. On a 650-trial suite of five
other shortcut mechanisms the primary judge abstains (\textsc{unclear}) on $80.6\%$
of trials. Where both commit they never contradict each other (\textbf{0/126},
$95\%$ upper bound $2.4\%$). What fails to transfer is
willingness to label rather than agreement. The abstention is also not independent
of the outcome, running $99\%$ on trials Claude calls \textsc{shortcut} against
$73\%$ elsewhere (Fisher $p = 4\times10^{-19}$). We exclude the suite entirely,
including a negative generalization result it appears to support, because analyzing
what remains would condition on the outcome
\citep{shi2025position, zheng2023judging}.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Semantic validity against human ratings}
\label{sec:sem}

We project the frozen probes onto EmoBank \citep{buechel2017emobank}, 10,062
human-rated sentences. The emotion vectors are frozen, but a ridge readout from the
50 projections to the valence, arousal and dominance ratings is fitted inside each
cross-validation fold, so the reported $R^2$ is out-of-fold rather than zero-shot.
Cross-validated $R^2$ is $\mathbf{0.377}$ for valence, $0.180$ for arousal and
$0.154$ for dominance. Unigram TF-IDF under the same folds and the same estimator
reaches $0.219$, $0.092$ and $0.063$. The probe clears the trivial text baseline. This supports construct validity
for an affect-related axis, not the label on any individual vector.

\subsection{Response length accounts for the preregistered association}
\label{sec:len}

On unsteered trials ($n = 120$, 14 events) the preregistered predictor performs
well, and so does character count. $V_{\text{int}}$[desperate] reaches AUC
$\mathbf{0.832}$ (bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.745, 0.908]$) and \texttt{len(response)}
reaches $\mathbf{0.888}$ $[0.787, 0.967]$.

Length does not \emph{beat} the probe (paired bootstrap $\Delta$AUC $+0.057$,
$[-0.080, +0.183]$). The problem is that the probe carries nothing else.
$\rho(V_{\text{int}}[\text{desperate}], \text{length}) = 0.606$
($p = 2.2\times10^{-13}$). Adding the probe to a length-only logistic model gives
$\chi^2(1) = 3.31$, $p = 0.069$. Residualizing on length leaves AUC
$= \mathbf{0.592}$ $[0.412, 0.756]$, which is a descriptive interval rather than a
test. The direction is not distinctive before that correction either, ranking 8th of
50 by raw AUC, behind \texttt{bored} at $0.985$. We read this as a measurement artifact rather than a substantive null. The
readout window spans the response, so a longer response mechanically changes
the average the probe projects.
This design cannot separate that from length mediating a genuine effect, in which
case residualizing over-adjusts. Either way the preregistered claim is not supported
as stated.

\subsection{A corrected sweep over all 50 directions}
\label{sec:dirs}

Length residualization does not empty the emotion subspace. We repeat the analysis
for all 50 directions with the nested likelihood-ratio test, asking whether a
direction adds to a model that already contains length. Selection is corrected by
max-$T$, whose reference distribution is that of the largest of the 50 statistics,
so significance is not an artifact of choosing the direction after seeing the
data.

Two design choices matter, and neither drives the result. The first is the
null. A free label permutation would test the \emph{marginal} hypothesis $y
\perp \text{direction}$, but length alone predicts the outcome at AUC $0.888$,
so we draw instead from a conditional null that preserves the outcome--length
relationship. The second is the penalty. With 14 events an unpenalized fit can
quasi-separate, so ridge-penalized estimates are primary, and there is no
complete separation. Both nulls give the same 18 directions with per-direction $p$
within $0.02$, and sweeping $\lambda \in \{0, 0.5, 1, 2\}$ moves the count only
between 16 and 18, leaving \texttt{desperate} null throughout ($p \le 0.50$).
Appendix~\ref{app:maxt} states the resampling procedure in full.

Eighteen of 50 directions are significant at $\alpha = .05$, and that count is
the fragile part. Monte Carlo noise alone moves it to 17 at $B = 2000$.
Changing the nuisance model moves it further, to 14 under a cubic-plus-knots
length basis and to 11 under fixed effects for the four task variants, whose
event rates are uneven at $9/30$ against $0/30$. Eleven directions clear all
three nuisance models. They are the low-arousal negative core plus \texttt{compassionate} and
\texttt{proud}, and what drops out is the positive-affect and high-arousal
periphery. \texttt{desperate} is null in all of them ($p \ge 0.43$).

\paragraph{The survivors are largely one shared axis.} They are heavily
intercorrelated, with mean $|r| = 0.76$, $78.8\%$ of the variance in the first
principal component, and a participation ratio of $1.58$. Low-arousal negative
affect sits at one pole and positive affect together with \texttt{compassionate}
at the other, with $\beta$ signs reversing accordingly. That is one axis detected many times, though it does not rule out further
structure.

\paragraph{The axis survives a direction-only readout, but its composition does not.}
Because the probe scales with activation magnitude (\S3), we repeated the sweep on
each trial's 50-vector normalized to unit length, which cancels the shared
$\lVert \bar a \rVert$ factor exactly. An association remains, but only 8 of the 18
survivors are shared, so whether the axis is best called valence or negative affect
turns on a measurement choice our preregistration mis-specified.
Appendix~\ref{app:readout} gives the numbers.

This exploratory association survives family-wise correction across the 50 tested
directions, and it is \emph{not the hypothesis we registered}. The correction handles selection among directions, not the fact that the sweep
was run after the registered direction failed. We do not assert that \texttt{bored} measures
boredom, since it may index an unmeasured response property in the way
\texttt{desperate} indexed length. The narrow claim is
that some axis in the emotion subspace carries information about reward hacking that length does not.

\subsection{Resolution limits of the text baseline}
\label{sec:vtext}

We had intended to compare $V_{\text{int}}$ against a judge-rated chain-of-thought
tone composite, $V_{\text{text}}$. That comparison is not evaluable here. On the
pooled set ($n = 992$, 87 events) $V_{\text{text}}$ holds one modal value for $74.0\%$ of trials, and its \texttt{frustration}
component is 1.0 in 985 of 992. As a result $43{,}485$ of $78{,}735$
positive--negative ROC pairs ($\mathbf{55.2\%}$) are ties contributing $0.5$
each.

$V_{\text{text}}$ therefore reaches AUC $0.647$ overall against $V_{\text{int}}$'s
$0.837$, but $\mathbf{0.836}$ on the 258 trials (22 events) where it is not at its
mode, against $0.890$ for $V_{\text{int}}$ on those same trials. On the trials it can
resolve it trails the probe by $0.054$ rather than $0.190$, so most of the gap is
tie compression in a non-responsive instrument rather than chain-of-thought
unfaithfulness. We withdraw any faithfulness-gap claim, including a quadrant
analysis an earlier draft built on this near-constant variable.

\subsection{Causal specificity against a matched-norm random null}
\label{sec:causal}

\begin{tabular}{@{}lccl@{}}
\toprule
Condition & hacks / $n$ & rate & vs.\ unsteered \\
\midrule
Unsteered baseline & 14 / 120 & 11.7\% & --- \\
Emotion vector @ $\pm0.3$ & 12 / 160 & 7.5\% & OR 0.61, $p = 0.30$ \\
5 random orthogonal directions @ $\pm0.3$ & 13 / 200 & 6.5\% & OR 0.53, $p = 0.14$ \\
\midrule
\multicolumn{4}{@{}l@{}}{\emph{Emotion vs.\ random: Fisher exact $p = 0.835$}} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\par}

Steering the emotion direction has no effect beyond what a matched arbitrary
direction produces. Neither arm differs from baseline, so this is not capability
degradation, and mean response lengths are 1402, 1324 and 1456 characters. The
dose--response over $\pm0.05$ to $\pm0.5$ is flat ($z = -1.42$, $p = 0.157$). A
``feel desperate'' system prompt also fails to separate from baseline ($17.5\%$ vs.\
$11.7\%$, Fisher $p = 0.417$). Pooling the random arm masks no heterogeneity. Its five directions have rates
of $0.025$ to $0.075$.

\paragraph{What this null does and does not exclude.} The emotion arm's risk ratio
against the random arm is $1.15$, $95\%$ CI $[0.54, \mathbf{2.46}]$, so the design rules out anything above about $2.4\times$ and leaves smaller
effects open. An $80\%$-power calculation agrees.
\citet{sofroniew2026} report $\sim\!14\times$, which is far outside that bound.
Their figure rests on a base rate we cannot match, since $14\times$ is unattainable
from our $11.7\%$, so the two compare on the relative scale only.

\paragraph{Why no layer rescues the registered direction.}
A null at one layer invites the objection that we picked the wrong site, and our
sweep appeared to supply it. On the 80-trial subset for which activations were
re-extracted (7 events), raw AUC for $V_{\text{int}}$[desperate] ranges from $0.450$
at layer 26 to $\mathbf{0.918}$ at layer 39, against $0.677$ at the steered layer.
Applying \S\ref{sec:len}'s control removes every layer's advantage, and length in
tokens, the units the probe averages over, alone reaches AUC $0.912$.

Layer 39's apparent superiority is the same length artifact, and its residualized CI
spans chance. \textbf{No evaluated layer gives the preregistered direction
length-independent signal detectable at $n = 80$}, so we have no observational
reason to move the intervention to a different site. Steering at a post-hoc site chosen on a confounded statistic is the failure
mode \citet{wu2026steerable} describe. We steered only at the registered
layer, so the specificity result covers that layer alone.

Layers 13 and 26 are significantly \emph{anti}-predictive after residualization, but
that is different geometry rather than a sign flip. Their full 50-direction
residualized-AUC profiles are nearly uncorrelated with layer 53's ($\rho = 0.24$ for
both), while layers 39, 52, 53 and 65 form a tightly correlated block (Spearman
$0.61$ to $0.99$). The behaviorally associated axis is a late-layer phenomenon, and
layer 39 correlates with the steered layer 53 at $\rho = 0.82$, so the two sites
largely measure the same thing.

\subsection{The preregistered generalization criterion remains unmet}
\label{sec:prereg}

The registration makes the leave-one-task-out mean load-bearing rather than pooled
AUC, and requires $\ge 6$ of 9 task variants to produce outcome variance, a guard
against between-task scale differences. The five
variants added to satisfy it are the ones \S3 excludes, because the primary judge
labels one of the five mechanisms and abstains on $\ge 98\%$ of the other four. The
verdict remains \textsc{insufficient-data}. The registration itself discloses being
drafted after an exploratory AUC of $0.900$, and we claim no pre-data standard.

\section{Limitations}

This is a single model, one task family in four variants, and 14 events, so the
inference is about these rollouts rather than reward hacking in general. The probe's
readout window includes the response, so \S\ref{sec:len} cannot separate measurement
artifact from mediation. Max-$T$ controls selection among directions, but not the possibility that the surviving directions index a further
unmeasured response property, and the survivor count is
sensitive to the nuisance model. The causal null is bounded at one steering norm and one layer.
No layer in our sweep offers a length-independent version of the registered
direction, but other token positions, extraction methods and norms remain open, and
we never steered the directions that \emph{do} survive \S\ref{sec:dirs}. Whether any
association generalizes across mechanisms is genuinely open, and we answer it from no data rather than from a subset our judge selected.

\section{Discussion}

\emph{Trivial-baseline controls belong beside random-direction controls.} The field
requires a steering effect to beat a random direction, but rarely requires a probe to beat a trivial feature of the output. Here a
character count did. \emph{Report the resolution of the instrument you
compare against.} A predictor constant for $74\%$ of trials gives no comparison at
all, so tie fractions belong beside AUCs. \emph{Correcting for selection can support
a result as well as remove one.} The same max-$T$ sweep that forbids the post-hoc
layer choice in \S\ref{sec:causal} licenses the one exploratory association we
report. These three steps test different inferential links, and passing one says
nothing about the next.
